# Trademark and Affiliation Policy (webette)

> **Note**: This document describes usage rules intended to prevent user confusion. It is not legal advice.

## 1) Purpose and definitions

The **webette Marks** (collectively) include:
- the name **"webette"** (word mark),
- the logo(s), the mascot, and any distinctive sign used to identify the project.

These webette Marks are used to indicate:
- the origin of the software,
- and/or **official** support or communication.

The goal of this policy is to:
- let the community talk about webette and build extensions and integrations,
- while protecting users against impersonation, confusion, and false claims of official status.

**Important**: "official" / "affiliated" includes (without limitation) any impression that you are the project, represent it, or are approved/sponsored by it.

## 2) What the licenses do not automatically allow

The source code is licensed under Apache-2.0. Documentation and artwork are licensed under CC BY 4.0 (see `LICENSING.md`). **These licenses are not a general permission to use the webette Marks as trademarks**.

In particular, CC BY 4.0 does not grant trademark rights.

## 3) General rules (anti-confusion)

You may use the webette Marks **without prior permission** if the use is:
- honest and non-misleading,
- not likely to cause confusion about origin, support, or affiliation,
- and does not suggest endorsement or approval.

When it makes sense (README, plugin page, marketplace listing, app description), add a clear non-affiliation statement (examples in section 8).

We want to stay **permissive**, especially for community and non-commercial uses; the limits below are mainly about preventing impersonation and confusion.

## 4) Allowed uses without permission (OK)

### 4.1 Nominative use (reference)
- Mention "webette" to describe the project (articles, videos, tutorials, comparisons).
- Say "compatible with webette" / "works with webette".
- Use "webette" in descriptive text (README, plugin documentation), as long as it is clear you are not the official project.

### 4.2 Logo/mascot in editorial contexts
You may use the logo or the mascot as an **illustration** (presentation, article, conference talk, review) if:
- it is not your primary branding,
- it does not imply an official relationship,
- and you do not use the logo/mascot as an app icon (see section 5).

Edits/adaptations (cropping, colored versions, compositions) are fine in an editorial context as long as they do not create confusion about official status.

### 4.3 Extensions, themes, plugins, integrations
- Name a project in a descriptive way, for example:
  - `webette-plugin-xyz` / `webette-theme-xyz`
  - "XYZ for webette"
  - `xyz-webette-integration`

...as long as you:
- do not give the impression that it is "official",
- add a statement such as: "Independent project, not affiliated with webette." or "Compatible with webette."
- avoid ambiguous wording (e.g., "webette Official Plugin", "webette Approved", "webette Certified").

You may also use a **modified version of the mascot** (including as an icon) for a project that works with webette if:
- the mascot is clearly **transformed** (style/colors/pose/elements) and cannot be confused with the official identity,
- your project name is at least as visible as the reference to webette,
- your project page includes a visible non-affiliation statement (section 8),
- and you follow the fork-specific rules if your project is a fork (section 6).

### 4.4 Community groups (meetups, forums, chat rooms)
You may create a community group around webette and use "webette" in the name **if**:
- the name clearly signals it is community-run (e.g., "webette Community FR", "Webette Meetup Paris (unofficial)"),
- the page/profile includes a visible non-affiliation statement,
- and you do not use a name that looks like an official channel (e.g., "support", "security", "status", "release", "core team").

For domain names and social handles, prefer a clear qualifier (e.g., `webette-community-fr`, `unofficial-webette-plugins`) and avoid "webette" alone.

### 4.5 Fan art and community merch (small scale)
Fan art is welcome. You may create, share, and sell fan art (illustrations, stickers, t-shirts, prints, etc.), including **modified** versions of the mascot, **without prior permission**, if:
- you do not claim (or suggest) that the product is official, approved, or sponsored by webette,
- you do not use "webette" as the **primary brand** of your shop/product line,
- you avoid confusion (e.g., do not use an identical copy of the official logo as the main mark of the product),
- sales remain **small-scale** (artists/individuals/small collectives; no industrial production; no mass distribution),
- and you comply with the applicable licenses for any assets you reuse (e.g., attribution is required if you reuse CC BY 4.0 resources).

## 5) Prohibited uses without permission (NO)

Without written permission, you may not:

- Claim or suggest that your product, service, site, account, or repository is **official**, **approved**, or **sponsored** by webette.
- Use the webette Marks as the **primary name** of your product/service (e.g., "webette Cloud", "webette Pro") or your company name.
- Register a domain name or social handle likely to create confusion (e.g., `webette-<service>.tld`, `@webette_support`, etc.).
- Use the **official logo** or the **official mascot** (unmodified) as an **app icon**, favicon, or **primary branding** for a fork or third-party product.
- Register (or attempt to register) a trademark, company name, product name, or domain name that appropriates the webette Marks or a confusingly similar variant.
- Use the webette Marks in paid ads or marketing in a way that implies an official relationship.
- Sell products while presenting them as "official merch", or at a scale likely to create confusion (mass production/distribution). (See the community exception in section 4.5.)

## 6) Forks, redistributions, and modified versions

Forks are allowed under the code license. We want this policy to be **open-source friendly**: what matters here is avoiding confusion for users.

### 6.1 Development / contribution forks (no distribution)
If your fork is for contributing, experimenting, or personal customization **without publishing a product** (no public binary/app/service, no marketing website), you can generally:
- keep the name "webette" in the repo (e.g., a GitHub fork) and in the description,
- keep the webette Marks in the code and the repo documentation,
- use "fork of webette" / "based on webette" (nominative use).

Recommended: add a visible non-affiliation statement in the README (section 8), especially if your fork is public.

### 6.2 Distributed forks (binaries, apps, services, public hosting)
If you **distribute** a modified version (release, package, app store listing, image, SaaS/public hosting, etc.), you must:
- **rename** the project (primary name) and avoid any presentation that looks like an official distribution,
- remove the webette Marks as primary branding (official logo/official mascot, favicon, app icon),
- clearly state "fork of webette" / "based on webette" in the description (nominative use), with an "unofficial" statement.

You may use a **modified mascot** for a distributed fork if:
- it is clearly transformed and cannot be confused with the official identity,
- the new fork name is the primary branding,
- and a visible non-affiliation statement is present (section 8).

### 6.3 Unmodified redistributions (build/repackaging)
If you redistribute an **unmodified** version (build/repackaging), you may use "webette" to identify the software as long as you:
- do not suggest official support,
- keep the applicable copyright/license notices,
- and do not use the webette Marks as the primary branding of your distribution.

## 7) Requesting permission

If you want a use that goes beyond the rules above (partnership, commercial integration, marketing usage, merch, event), contact:

- pro@webette.org

Please include:
- the planned use,
- the visuals,
- the geographic scope,
- and the duration.

## 8) Recommended statements (anti-affiliation)

Recommended for public contexts (README, plugin page, marketplace listing, site):

> "webette" is an open-source project. [Name-of-the-Project] is an independent project, not affiliated with webette.  
> Official link: https://webette.org

Acceptable short variants:
- "Compatible with webette. Not affiliated."
- "Community plugin for webette (unofficial)."

If you reuse assets that require attribution (e.g., CC BY 4.0), add an appropriate attribution notice (author/source/license), for example on the project page or product listing.

## 9) Changes to this policy

This policy may evolve. If a use conflicts with this document's anti-confusion purpose, the project team may ask you to change (or stop) the use to avoid ambiguity.
